Round HUD redesign

Target: a 5-inch 1080×1080 round panel (≈216 ppi) read from an armchair, 60–90 cm away, operated with a finger. Everything below is drawn at exactly half size; the annotations are real pixels.

≥ 96 pxminimum tap target (≈11 mm). Today's HUD uses 36–48 px — a fingertip covers two of them.
≥ 24 pxminimum readable text at 60–90 cm. Current 10–11 px labels are illegible from a chair.
r ≤ 430 pxcontent band. Anything outside the inner circle gets cut by the bezel or lands at an awkward angle.
4 screensNow Playing, Library, Queue, Settings. Today's six modes + five overlays + a radial carousel are the lag and the confusion.

Screens

Interaction changes that matter more than looks

  1. Four screens, one gesture language. Now Playing · Library · Queue · Settings. Bottom pill tabs (92 px) on list screens; on Now Playing the two diameter buttons. Swipe left/right anywhere = next/previous track; swipe down on a list = back. No more modes that look alike (activePlayer / audiophileDeck / centerpiece / settingsMenu / drawers / carousel).
  2. Clock is a screensaver, not a mode. After the idle timeout the Now Playing screen dims to the clock as an overlay; one tap brings it back in under 150 ms because nothing is unmounted (the video surface stays live, just hidden).
  3. Every tap answers instantly. Press state within one frame, the request goes to the daemon after. Rows highlight on touch-down, buttons scale, a 2 px progress line under the header shows "working" instead of a spinner in the middle.
  4. Buffering is visible and honest. Casting or a Drive track shows "Downloading · 42 %" where the seek bar is, and the previous track keeps playing until the new one is ready.
  5. Text contrast ≥ 7:1 everywhere (white on near-black, muted grey no lighter than #8f9ab0). No 10–11 px labels, no 40 % opacity text.
  6. Volume is a single ring gesture: drag around the rim of the disc (the one gesture a round screen is uniquely good at). Shown as a thin arc at the edge for 1.5 s, driven by the DAC's own volume register.
  7. Fewer colours, used consistently. Cyan = YouTube/video, gold = Hi-Fi, green = live/healthy, red = error. The dominant-colour tint from album art stays, but only on the background glow.
