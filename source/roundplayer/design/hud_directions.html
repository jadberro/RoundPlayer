What changes. The cover fills the disc; a dark gradient only where the text and controls sit. The record becomes a translucent groove ring over the art, so the album stays visible through it. Play button, seek fill and the format readout take the album's own accent (here the warm gold pulled from the art); the fixed gold/cyan survive only as the little source label. Manrope 800 for the title.

  • Looks different for every album, which is what a music player should do.
  • Video gets the identical frame with the picture instead of the cover, so the two modes feel like one product.
  • Busy covers are tamed by the bottom gradient, not by blur.
GPU: one image + two gradients + a rotated ring → same cost as today.
Work: ~1 day. Accent extraction already exists (daemon dominant colour).
Risk: low-contrast covers need the fallback accent; handled by a luminance check.

What changes. A brushed-metal rim at the bezel, enamel-black face, a big record with a real label and a tonearm that swings onto it when you press play. The seek bar becomes an arc along the lower edge, the one control a round screen does better than a rectangle. Two VU arcs left and right follow the audio level (mpv reports it; 10 Hz is plenty). Sora for the title, IBM Plex Mono for every readout, like a front panel.

  • Reads as a device you bought, not an app you installed. Fits the DAC Pro story.
  • The arc seek and VU meters give the rim a reason to exist.
  • Video mode drops the record and arm but keeps rim, arc and meters.
GPU: vectors + one rotated image; VU arcs repaint a 150 px box at 10 Hz → fine.
Work: ~2 days (tonearm, arc seek with touch mapping, level feed from mpv).
Risk: arc seek is harder to scrub precisely; keep tap-to-seek and the ±10 s double-tap.

How it combines. A's full-bleed cover and translucent groove ring on the upper half; C's typography owns the lower half: the title in Bebas Neue at 124 px, left-aligned on the chord, artist and album in a light weight on one line with "up next" tucked after it, a mono readout line above. Controls are C's monochrome: white play, outlined prev/next, no gold, no cyan. The only colour on the screen is the album's.

  • Album art gives every track its own look; the big white title keeps it legible over any cover.
  • Nothing is centred except the transport, so the eye has a start point (top-left of the title) and the disc stops feeling like a clock face.
  • Titles longer than the chord auto-shrink to 96 px, then ellipsis; two lines never happen, the artist line stays put.
GPU: image + two gradients + rotated ring + text → same as today.
Work: ~1.5 days incl. the screensaver and video variants below, Bebas Neue + Inter Tight bundled.
Risk: Bebas has no lowercase; mixed-case titles become caps, which suits most but not all ("iTunes Session"). Fallback: Oswald 600 if you'd rather keep case.

My pick now: D

The hybrid keeps everything that made A right (the art does the work, free on the GPU) and fixes A's one weakness: a centred Manrope title over a bright cover can get lost. The condensed white title at 124 px never loses. It also gives the screensaver and the player the same voice, which no other option did.

A is the one that still looks good with the thousandth album, because the album does the work. The rules that keep it clean are already in the code: the content band, the bottom gradient, finger-sized buttons. It costs nothing on the GPU and the accent extraction exists in the daemon.

From B, take the arc seek along the lower edge and the brushed rim. The arc gives the round panel its own gesture without the volume-ring problem (it is a display you can tap, not a gesture you can trigger by accident), and the rim makes the bezel look intentional instead of like a cut-off. Skip the tonearm and the VU meters for now; they are the kind of thing that looks great in a render and busy at 8 p.m.

C stays as the screensaver: the big condensed clock, and when something is playing, the title in the same face underneath it. That is where its calm belongs.
